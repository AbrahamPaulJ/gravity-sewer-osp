Peak factor1.0
Manning's n per material

Pipe roughness. 0.010 to 0.011 is new PVC, 0.013 the design value for concrete and vitrified clay, 0.015 and above is old or root-affected pipe. It is the only term in Manning's equation we cannot look up: the statewide layer publishes a roughness field on this network on no record at all. By default n comes from each pipe's published material, 0.010 for uPVC against 0.013 for clay; drag the slider to override that with one value everywhere, and press auto to go back. Watch the water level move, because that sensitivity is the argument for asking the network operator what n they design to.

View

Elevation exaggeration30x
Real elevation change is tiny next to the map's width, so height is stretched by this factor to make upstream and downstream visibly different. Drag to rotate, scroll to zoom, right-drag to pan.
Pipe bore exaggeration70x
Needs the "Capacity, d/D" colouring. A 300 mm pipe is invisible next to a 1 km wide region, so the bore is stretched like the elevation is. The water level inside it is the normal depth Manning gives for the flow that reach carries, drawn as the true circular segment, and the scroll speed is v = Q/A from the same solution. Steady uniform flow only: no backwater, no storage, no time.

Long-section

A side view down the sewer: ground level on top, manhole shafts as verticals, the pipe stepping down between them, and the water inside it. Click any chamber on the map to cut a section through it. The route follows the largest tributary upstream and the trunk downstream, which is the way the flow goes.

Route length12 each way
Chambers traced upstream and downstream of the one you clicked. Keep it short. The vertical scale is the horizontal scale times the exaggeration, so a longer route squashes everything: trace the full 4 km through Walkerville and a 300 mm pipe is a quarter of a pixel tall, with the water invisible inside it. A real long-section covers a few hundred metres for the same reason.
Vertical exaggeration45x
A sewer falls a few metres over hundreds, so at true scale the pipe is a flat line. Drawing offices conventionally use 10x, but that assumes a short route on a large sheet: here it needs to be higher to keep the bore visible. The factor is printed on the drawing, because a long-section without it stated is a misleading picture.
Pipe bore exaggeration1.0x
Leave at 1x for a true drawing. Raise it only to make a small pipe's water level readable, and say so if you screenshot it.
