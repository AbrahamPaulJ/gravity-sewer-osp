Simulation 3's greenfield runs: a new estate of 10, 30, 80, 150 or 700 homes joins at each of 328 manholes, under eight weather cases, solved in EPA SWMM. The first ten sensors for each rule are the greedy set Simulation 3 publishes (this page reproduces its order and scores exactly); later sensors continue the same method: each one is the manhole that most raises the share seen in the worst weather case. Simulation 3's recommended set comes from its infill runs and the exact solver, so it can differ from this order. Four weather cases (Dry, Design wet and its two variants) are still the fixed 480-minute runs, not re-run until levels settle; they are marked in the weather list. The hydraulic model is uncalibrated, so these are model estimates, not field results.

Why a sensor can see nothing in one view: each sensor is chosen to lift the worst weather case, so some only work in wet weather or for large estates. Under the 10 mm rule at 150 homes, sensor 1 (manhole 4454116) runs 0.15 to 0.25 m deep in dry and design wet weather and sees no estate there, but once it surcharges in beyond-design weather it sees 219. Sensor 2 (manhole 1629194) sees almost nothing below 700 homes, and 313 of the 700-home estates in dry weather. Simulation 2 showed a third reason: a manhole just downstream that already spills sends the extra flow out as overflow instead of raising the level upstream.
