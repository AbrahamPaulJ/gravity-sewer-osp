Which sensor sees growth where · Simulation 3, greenfield growth

Sensor placement read backwards: placement chooses sensors from the growth they see; this page starts from the sensors and shows the manholes they cover. Each dot is a manhole where a new estate joins the network in Simulation 3, coloured by the level sensor that detects that growth under the chosen rule. Sensors are added in Simulation 3's greedy order, for the best worst weather case or the best average over the eight, carried on past its ten until every detectable run is seen. Simulation 3's greenfield runs: a new estate of 10, 30, 80, 150 or 700 homes joins at each of 328 manholes, under eight weather cases, solved in EPA SWMM. The first ten sensors for each rule and objective are the greedy sets Simulation 3 publishes (this page reproduces their order and scores exactly); later sensors continue the same method: each one is the manhole that most raises the share seen in the worst weather case (best worst case) or on average over the eight (best average), all five estate sizes counted. "All weather cases" shows a manhole as covered when a placed sensor sees its growth in at least one case, and outlines it more faintly when some cases where it shows are missed. Simulation 3's recommended set comes from its infill runs and the exact solver, so it can differ from this order. Four weather cases (Dry, Design wet and its two variants) are still the fixed 480-minute runs, not re-run until levels settle; they are marked in the weather list. The hydraulic model is uncalibrated, so these are model estimates, not field results.

Why a sensor can see nothing in one view: each sensor is chosen over all eight weather cases, so some only work in wet weather or for large estates. In the best-worst-case set under the 10 mm rule at 150 homes, sensor 1 (manhole 4454116) runs 0.15 to 0.25 m deep in dry and design wet weather and sees no estate there, but once it surcharges in beyond-design weather it sees 219. Sensor 2 (manhole 1629194) sees almost nothing below 700 homes, and 313 of the 700-home estates in dry weather. Simulation 2 showed a third reason: a manhole just downstream that already spills sends the extra flow out as overflow instead of raising the level upstream.
